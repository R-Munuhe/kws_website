History of Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS)

The Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) was officially established in 1989 by an Act of Parliament to protect and manage Kenya's rich biodiversity.

Key Historical Milestones

• Pre-Colonial and Colonial Period: Indigenous communities practiced sustainable land use. Later, colonial laws and sport hunting led to major wildlife declines.
• 1945–1946: The Royal National Parks Ordinance was passed, and Nairobi National Park was established as the country's first national park.
• 1963–1977: Following independence, Kenya recognized wildlife as a national heritage, culminating in a national hunting ban in 1977.
• 1970–1989: A severe rhino and elephant poaching crisis hit the country, reducing the black rhino population in Tsavo from over 8,000 to fewer than 20.
• 1989: KWS was founded under the leadership of conservation icons like Richard Leakey, who famously led massive ivory and rhino horn burnings to draw global attention to poaching.
